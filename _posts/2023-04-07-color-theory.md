Are the colours we use in our artworks casual or perfectly studied? How can we decide the colour? In the next few articles, we'll try to discover and understand the aspects of this topic.

### **What's the colour theory?**

The rules about the use of colours are called colour theory. These rules are used in different areas, such as advertising or graphics.

## **The primary color**

The idea behind the original graphic design was that we were able to generate whatever kind of colour we wanted by using the three fundamental colours, called the **primary colour**s. There was:

- Red
- Yellow
- Blue

By mixing these 3 main colours, we could create the **secondary colou**r:

- Orange (Red+Yellow)
- Purple (Red+Blue)
- Green (Yellow+Blue)

[![COlor wheel](/assets/images/2023/09/cropped-SPHERE.png)](/assets/images/2023/09/cropped-SPHERE.png)

In the end the tertiary colours were the fusion of secondary and primary together.

## **The color wheel**

During history painters tried to understand how these colours work. The study of colour has become more complex due to the addition of new variables:

- Hue
- Chroma
- Lighting

The theory changed everything with these new parameters. At this point a new category of colour was added. The achromatic colours:

- White
- Grey
- Black

Colours that are right opposite each other in the colour wheel cancel out each other's hues, according to a conjecture made by Newton; this idea was more proven scientifically in the 19th century. The new discovery changed the colour wheel, putting the white at the centre of the wheel.
