## A History of usefulness

Bronze is a special metal, and it can recall its importance from ancient times (3300 B.C. to 1100 B.C.) during the so-called "Bronze Age."

The first to discover it were probably the Sumerians, who used it for tools around 3300 B.C.

The most common theory suggests that the discovery of bronze was totally accidental, probably during the creation of a campfire ring from stones with a high concentration of copper and tin. It is theorised that the heat from the fire melted these materials, creating the first alloy in the history of humankind.

From its discovery the use of this new material spread around the world and continued until 1100 B.C., when the Bronze Age was substituted by the Iron Age, with iron being easier to produce and stronger as a metal.

[![An image of a table with different objects made of bronze.](/assets/images/2025/07/Bronze-utilities-320x480.jpg)](/assets/images/2025/07/Bronze-utilities-320x480.jpg)

The use of bronze returned to popularity during the Greek and Roman periods, when the metal was used in the creation of ships and statues. Due to the presence of copper in the alloy, the oxidation helped to preserve the statues and the structures.

Then around 800 A.C., with the invention of gunpowder, the Chinese used bronze in their first firearms. After the Europeans used bronze for bullets.

In our day the bronze is used in different applications, but the most iconic ones are the medal's material and religious statues.

## Bronze: a symbol of luxury and functionality

Although bronze was one of the first metals to be used and found, we normally consider it a precious material. The main reason for this is the similarity with gold, another shiny metallic material used as a symbol in competitions.

Because of its high reliability, the bronze quickly became a symbol of stability and maturity too.

At the same time, the low rarity of this alloy gave a secondary position to this colour in contrast with the high value perceived from his relatively similar alternatives, [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">gold </span>](/gold-psychology/)and [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">silver</span>](/color-psychology-of-silver/). This middle position of less importance is principally a consequence of the Olympics. In the Olympic sports, the podium shows the best three players on the field, although the importance of the role, bronze is the symbol of the last on the podium. This symbolism gave a bad reputation to this colour that became both a symbol of luxury because of its association with silver and gold and, at the same time, a symbol of delusion and defeat.

## Child of orange and brown

As we have already seen in another article (check [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">orange</span>](/color-orange/)), all the colours that derive from or are similar in some aspect to other colours inherit parts of their symbolism and significance. This time the colour bronze resembles [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">gold</span>](/gold-psychology/), brown, and [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">orange</span>](/color-orange/).

Lighter hues of bronze (the alloys with a higher percentage of copper) resemble more the vivacity of orange, while the darker hues resemble more the earthiness and stability typically associated with brown. In the end we have the more shiny and balanced hues of bronze that resemble the luxury often associated with [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">gold</span>](/gold-psychology/).

## Personality traits of bronze

Usually various cultures associate specific moods or personality traits with colours. The particularity in this case is the wide range of differences generated by this peculiar colour that can be both shiny and opaque at the same time.

A key characteristic of bronze is the timeless feeling it provokes. As we said before, the oxidation of copper was known even in ancient times; precisely this physical property adds the idea of a timelessness.

In addition, the high usage of this metal gave the idea of stability, but at the same time, it suggests a selfless helper. A bronze person usually is used to represent a person who helps independently from their needs.

Despite these positive connotations, some people associate bronze with a brutally honest character too. In other words, bronze represents a character that is both precious and reliable, even though his character can be a little tough depending on the circumstance.

To conclude, the connection with [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">orange</span>](http://orange)<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)"> </span>and brown gave a more "human" nature to this colour, despite the connection with luxury given from metals like gold and silver.
