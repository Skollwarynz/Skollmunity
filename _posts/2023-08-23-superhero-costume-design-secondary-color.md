## **Green**

In heroes, the presence of [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">green</span>](/color-green/) is related to nature; normally, this colour represents secondary heroes. Even though sometimes we have villains all green with power that are usually related to nature, an example of that is [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">Poison Ivy</span>](https://batman.fandom.com/wiki/Poison_Ivy).

[![Hand in nature 320x223](/assets/images/2023/08/hand_in_nature-320x223.jpg)](https://www.freepik.com/)

## **Purple and green**

Villain designs usually use a combination of these secondary colours. When drawers use these colours, they are trying to fuse the creativity of purple with the natural growth connection of green.

[![clown](/assets/images/2023/08/clown-225x480.png)](https://www.freepik.com/)

## **Purple and orange**

Even purple and orange are popular combinations of hues. In this case, the creativity of purple is fused with the energy of [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">orange </span>](/color-orange/)(as we said [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">here</span>](/color-orange/)).

The most iconic example of this silly and joyful power is the [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">Joker</span>](https://dc.fandom.com/wiki/Joker). This villain possesses every secondary colour (purple, orange, and green), making him one of the most powerful and enduring villains.

[![Card of the joker](/assets/images/2023/08/joker_poker-320x434.jpg)](https://www.freepik.com/)

## **Blue and purple**

![Woman with a purple and blue costume](/assets/images/2023/08/purple-and-blue-costume.jpg-313x480.png)

Blue and purple is maybe the strangest combination: it takes a primary and a secondary colour and fuses them together. I suggest you read the article about blue by clicking [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">here</span>](/color-blue/)<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)"> </span>because the symbols used are explained better. In fact, the wisdom of blue is connected with the nobility of purple, creating an apathetic villain. An example of this design is [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">Galactus</span>](https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Galactus), the Devourer of the World.

## **Yellow and green**

Even this combination uses a primary and secondary colour. In this case, the mix suggests powers that derive from nature or mystical sources. Examples of this design are [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">Loki</span>](https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Loki_Laufeyson_(Earth-616)), the god of illusion, or [<span class="mark has-inline-color has-vivid-purple-color" style="background-color:rgba(0, 0, 0, 0)">Electro</span>](https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Electro).
