## A quick recap on the story

The story of Whiplash follows a young jazz musician, Andrew. We viewers follow him through the path of Andrew into becoming the best drummer in the world. Unfortunately this process is fused with anxiety and obsession, and it finishes with Andrew reaching his goal but with the price of his soul and life attached to this.

## Dichotomy of color

The film is shot with mainly two hues, yellow and green. The use of just two main colors to drive the viewer's feeling using common associations. <a href="/effect-of-yellow-on-our-mind/" data-id="2742" data-type="post">Yellow</a> and <a href="/color-orange/" data-id="2656" data-type="post">orange</a> are used when Andrew feels joy and ambition. This association is firstly presented when Andrew watches the band where he wants to play, and here the colors have these specific hues.

In contrast, when he goes to his room, we can see how the colors shift to more green and grey tones. This choice is used to make the viewer empathize with Andrew and understand how he doesn't feel like he fits with "normal" musicians.

From the first shot, the film expresses Andrew's isolation. We start with a long shot in a greeny hue that instantly expresses Andrew's discomfort while practicing for his dream. Even though the allay is greeny, the room with Andrew is <a href="/color-blue/" data-id="2555" data-type="post">blue</a> as a symbol of calm.

This calm is then interrupted by Fletcher's arrival. This scene is used to represent the role of Fletcher for the rest of the film. As we see him controlling Andrew's performance, his approval will become the base for the toxic relationship between the two characters. His person is presented in a black background with a black suit as a symbol of authority over Andrew.

From this scene the film starts his narration, and we'll see how the contrast between green and yellow tells a story with cinematic angles.

## Three division acts

So let's start the analysis with a deeper focus on the storytelling. The story can be divided into three main acts, and each one is a core step into the transformation of Andrew.

### Act 1:

During the first act we have the presentation of Andrew, a normal guy with an ambitious dream, becoming the best drummer in the world. Right from the start we notice that his ambition is not just a facade but a lifestyle; we start with him practicing, and most of the moments with Andrew alone are connected with practice. In the first act the main color is yellow and orange, as the main character is excited. During the first act we have the first confrontation with Fletcher. The calm is instantly cracked by Fletcher's arrival, and so Adnrew's ambition is crushed. During this first act we meet the father of Andrew, a probably weak man, as we can see from the idea of giving up on Andrew's dream and the apology in the cinema.

During these first shots, dark colors represent bad moments, and I personally think that the transformation of these dark hues into more green hues during the film is a perfect representation of the toxicity that Andrew inherits during the film. An important disclaimer must be done: all the story is told through Andrew's point of view. For this reason, the father is shown as weak, or the colors are joyful during the first shots. Even though the main focus of the first act is to present the main character, his dreams, and post an important question: **"Can Andrew reach his** dream?"

At the end of the first act we have the response as Andrew starts to change and is welcomed into the band. The first point of change is presented when Andrew asks the cinema girl out, a change of spirit that was used to make the viewer cheer with Andrew as he became the person capable of reaching his dream.

### Act 2:

During the second act the yellow color starts to fade and transitions to a more orangey green. During this second act the main character is presented with different obstacles, and we have the first point of break. The character starts to obliterate his identity and change in order to be enough. In the case of Andrew, he started to become more and more similar to Fletcher. For this reason the clothes of Andrew start to become more and more black as a resemblance to Fletcher. Another important change is presented by the other secondary color of the film, <a href="/color-red-psychological-effects/" data-id="2493" data-type="post">red</a>.

Red represents the literal blood that Andrew spills while practicing so hard to hurt himself. This color represents the danger and the violence that Andrew's character is taking. This act destroys the illusion we thought we had as Andrew.

The first example is during the first musical performance of Andrew. Fletcher starts his manipulation by letting Andrew show his vulnerability, like the profession of the father and his dream. Then after creating a safe space, he proceeds to destroy Andrew's confidence with the famous scene "Are you dragging or are you rushing?"

An important detail to note is that during these scenes the yellow hue of before became more and more green as we felt Andrew's discomfort.

Another reason for this discomfort is his perception of the angles: as the scene continues, Fletcher starts to invade a larger section of the screen, and we as viewers can feel the anxiety and grief of Andrew.

The scene concludes with the necessity of more practice from Andrew. This is the breaking point; from this moment on, Andrew starts his transformation.

The second act is important because it continuously dissolves the viewer. As Andrew improves, the difficulties continue; for example, Fletcher decides to call a new drummer. This decision points to the question again, "Is Andrew good enough?" With this continued anxiety and tension with the narration, we gradually obsess and fall into the trap as Andrew. When he starts to think that he can do it, we cheer for him. In the end we reach the complete annihilation of his identity as Andrew breaks up with the girlfriend and has the car crash scene. At this point Andrew's obsession is so strong that even the viewer has a problem with him. Now the story is ready for the third and final act.

### Act 3

A desaturated point of view introduces this act. In addition to Andrew's grief, we have a strong contrast; the used-to-brung colors and the normal hues make the scene more sad and boring. That's the idea. As Andrew, we feel the necessity to return to vibrant color, so we want Andrew to return to playing drums. So when Fletcher proposes Andrew to play Whiplash one more time, we cheer with him.

That's a perfect moment for the final tension, when Fletcher shows to Andrew that he knows what he did. In this moment Andrew's escape scene makes the viewer grieve with Andrew. So we finally reach the end, where Fletcher's approval becomes the real prize. An expensive one, as the father seeing the son from a distance is the representation of how much Andrew had to sacrifice to reach his goal, twisted from playing drums to Fletcher's approval.

## Primary colors of the film: yellow and green

As already said, yellow and green are used during the film as main hues to tell the story and make the viewer empathize with Andrew's life. Yellow and orange resemble the instruments' color and so the joy of playing jazz. This specific type of hue helps the viewer feel and relax during the film, helping the creation of continuous spanning and tension points during the narration.

We see yellow when ambition and joy dominate the scene. This technique builds comfort and false hope, which are perfect for Fletcher's aggression after. This color is undoubtedly the main character during the first half of the film, and he is often used as a contrast with more chaotic or boring scenes to express Andrew's excitement.

A perfect example is presented by the contrast between the music scene in the normal class and the Fletcher class. When in a normal class, students have confused movements. Editing is calm and not dynamic; instead, during Fletcher's class, we have a dynamic cutting and continuous change of scene that reflects the music and control of the class. As for the color, we can see how Fletcher's class has a yellow tone, while the normal class doesn't.

Green plays the role of contrast with the yellow hue. Green is used to represent discomfort and make the viewer feel sick, like Andrew during tension or when he's late for an important task. As the film continues, the presence of green increments increases, and this represents how the environment became more and heavier to endure.

## Secondary colors: black and red

In addition to the main dualism, whiplash presents another dualism too. The secondary main colors are <a href="/heroes-design-secretslight-and-dark-colors/" data-id="2917" data-type="post">black and red</a> (we already talked of this color in superhero costumes too). Red is mainly represented by the blood during the show; when presented, we find Andrew during a difficult situation: the car crash and the intense training session. But its meaning shows rivalry too; if we focus on his classmate, we'll see that his red hair and red shirts are used to represent the perfect rival for Andrew. Each Andrew is in a difficult position and is not the best drummer; this character appears as another challenge to overcome for our main character.

Instead, Black has the role of authority. Each time in the story when people wear black, that means that the character either is important in the room or has the idea of being powerful. For example, Fletcher always wears black as a symbol of his authority. For this reason, during the jazz bar scene, it is the only time we don't see him wearing black clothes. Other times when Black people appear are during concerts.

The only other time we see Black is during the breakup scene, where Andrew has become completely a little Fletcher.

## Conclusion

This film has a lot of hidden symbolism, and for this reason most people have issues completely understanding it. For example, the director viewed the conclusion as a seductive conclusion, while a lot of fans think otherwise.

Some misrepresentation of jazz made people dislike the film. That's because the film presents a stereotypical representation of the music genre. The story was inspired by the bad memory of the director from when he studied jazz. As a matter of fact, the song Whiplash itself was taught to the director too.

To conclude, we can argue about the real meaning and emotion that film made us feel, but one thing is secure: Whiplash hides a lot of meaning under the already intense story.
